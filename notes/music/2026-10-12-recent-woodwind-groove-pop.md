## Closest matches

### Sessa — Pequena Vertigem de Amor
- Released **November 7, 2025**; 9 tracks.
- Qobuz: https://open.qobuz.com/album/frq4533amkodc
- Why: the earlier stripped-down Brazilian approach has become a lush ensemble of strings, flute, electric piano, warm bass, female vocal harmonies and subtle percussion. A stronger match to Cohen's orchestral softness than Grandeza; rhythmic detail prevents it becoming inert.
- First tracks: **Dodói**, then **Nome de Deus**. For instrumental flute, **Roupa Dos Mortos**.
- Caveat: Portuguese-language male lead; not V.V.'s angular/wry art-pop side.
- Review evidence: Uncut calls it cosmic samba-soul and describes the fuller instrumentation; Joyzine specifically identifies Gabriel Milliet's flute, string swells, ghostly backing vocals and understated drums. AllMusic stresses the tension between soft sounds and rich arrangements.
- Sources: https://www.uncut.co.uk/reviews/album/sessa-pequena-vertigem-de-amor-reviewed-brazilian-playboy-is-born-again-152062/ ; https://joyzine.org/2025/11/09/album-review-sessa-pequena-vertigem-de-amor/ ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/pequena-vertigem-de-amor-mw0004619778

### Pearl & The Oysters — Monkey Mind
- Released **June 26, 2026**; 12 tracks.
- Qobuz: https://open.qobuz.com/album/cgj0312z3hlf3
- Why: full-band, live-to-tape recording with Jonathan Rado takes them toward earthier '70s soft rock while keeping electric piano, flute, harmonies, Brazilian funk/samba and city-pop movement. Bright music explicitly carries anxious/dark lyrics, unusually close to Practice Smiling's emotional contrast.
- First tracks: **Stratford & 52**, then **Doom Mood**; **Wide Awake** for the tender side.
- Caveat: still more whimsical/retro-futurist than Cohen.
- Sources: https://post-trash.com/news/2026/8/25/pearl-amp-the-oysters-monkey-mind-album-review ; https://www.allmusic.com/album/monkey-mind-mw0004806905 ; https://floodmagazine.com/226075/pearl-the-oysters-monkey-mind/

### Dina Ögon — Människobarn
- Released **February 6, 2026**; 13 tracks, double LP.
- Qobuz: https://open.qobuz.com/album/nqg07pbk9dnpb
- Why: sophisticated, warm soul-pop, jazz and yacht-rock elements; honeyed guitar, light drums, Minnie Riperton-like melodic contours and a more melancholy emotional atmosphere than Oas. A rhythm-section and harmony match, not primarily a flute match.
- First tracks: **Grå labyrint**, then **Dålig teve**.
- Caveat: longer, more polished, Swedish-language record. Reviews disagree on the lyrics: Fokus Musik admires the playing but strongly criticizes the writing.
- Sources: https://www.aftonbladet.se/nojesbladet/musik/a/y57dXx/en-melankolisk-skymning-har-fallit-over-dina-ogon ; https://fokusmusik.se/dina-ogon-manniskobarn/ ; https://evl.one/manniskobarn-by-dina-ogon

## Other worthwhile branches

### ALA.NI — Sunshine Music
- Released **September 19, 2025**; 11 tracks.
- Qobuz: https://open.qobuz.com/album/ea0tuw6uxbgdb
- Why: intimate vocal arrangements, chunky bass, light percussion, horns and woodwind, with calypso, jazz and bossa nova. Sweet musical surfaces carry difficult relationships and political messages.
- First tracks: **Something You Said**, then **Summer Meadows**; **TIEF** for the bossa/political contrast.
- Caveat: more Caribbean/classic vocal-pop than psychedelic indie; a neighbouring branch.
- Sources: https://atthebarrier.com/2025/09/23/ala-ni-sunshine-music-album-review/ ; https://lesoreillescurieuses.com/2025/09/19/ala-ni-sunshine-music/

### Lau Ro — Lau
- Released **June 26, 2026**; 10 tracks.
- Qobuz: https://open.qobuz.com/album/dev9md9toykhh
- Why: Brazilian folk/MPB harmony inside tender orchestral psych-pop; acoustic guitar, lush strings, harmonized vocals, subtle percussion, reverb piano and cinematic interludes.
- First tracks: **Feel** for jazzy rhythmic movement, then **Conclusão** for tenderness.
- Caveat: quieter and more diffuse than Practice Smiling; more Cohen's pastoral side than V.V.'s pop snap.
- Sources: https://kristanreed.substack.com/p/review-lau-rolau-2026 ; https://www.theselfportraitgospel.com/reviews-premiers/lau-ro-lau ; https://aquariumdrunkard.com/2026/06/18/lau-ro-lau/
- Access boundary: search returned substantial Kristan Reed review text, but rendered page exposed only a short preview. Aquarium Drunkard supplied a public excerpt only. Supplemented by the Self Portrait Gospel feature and Qobuz description; no paywall was bypassed.

### Laure Briard — Voyage Mental
- Released **June 5, 2026**; 13 tracks.
- Qobuz: https://open.qobuz.com/album/kq7v4hub4vxx5
- Why: her newer album combines French yé-yé, Californian psych-folk, soft-rock colours and bittersweet introspection. More reflective, folky and mature than the 2023 record recommended previously.
- First tracks: **Rocking Chair**, then **Force Invisible**.
- Caveat: less of a Brazilian groove match than Ne pas trop rester bleue. Don't transfer every instrument descriptor from that earlier album onto this one.
- Sources: https://lesoreillescurieuses.com/2026/06/05/laure-briard-voyage-mental/ ; https://www.telerama.fr/musique/voyage-mental-de-laure-briard-une-delicieuse-deambulation-en-chanson_cri-7044584.php ; https://www.faceszine.com/single-post/laure-briard-sentiment-pop
- Access boundary: Télérama preview used only; full Les Oreilles Curieuses review and Faces interview supplied additional context.

### TOPS — Bury the Key
- Released **August 22, 2025**; 12 tracks.
- Qobuz: https://open.qobuz.com/album/q85cw5o3wfa9a
- Why: breathy singing, melodic bass, shimmering synths, disco movement and Jane Penny's flute carry darker subject matter. A strong alternative if what matters is pop groove/unease rather than the Brazilian component.
- First tracks: **Outstanding in the Rain**, then **Your Ride**.
- Caveat: familiar artist, new album in the checked history. More '80s city-pop/synth-pop; Spectrum Culture finds the album too uniform, while Still Listening is substantially more positive.
- Sources: https://spectrumculture.com/2025/09/01/tops-bury-the-key-review/ ; https://www.stilllisteningmagazine.com/reviews/tops-bury-the-key-review ; https://montrealrocks.ca/album-review-tops-bury-the-key/
