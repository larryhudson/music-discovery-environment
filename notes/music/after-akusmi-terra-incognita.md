## Verified seed profile

**Akusmi — *Terra Incognita***  
**July 3, 2026 · Tonal Union · seven digital tracks, approximately 47:45**  
[Official Bandcamp, including credits](https://akusmi.bandcamp.com/album/terra-incognita) · [Qobuz](https://play.qobuz.com/album/nsdiyrzmudugg)

Akusmi is French-born, London-based composer **Pascal Bideau**. Crucially, this album is not merely a continuation of *Fleeting Future*’s meticulously stacked electroacoustic minimalism. The official notes emphasize **Afrobeat, highlife and electronic Afro-pop**, with a looser, earthier sound. [The Quietus](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/akusmi-terra-incognita-review/) similarly describes patient repetition and rubbery, relaxed grooves rather than mechanized precision.

### The musical dimensions worth following

- **Minimalism as a social groove:** patterns remain legible while other parts circle around them. [Pitchfork’s review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/akusmi-terra-incognita/) distinguishes the debut’s upward-building structures from this album’s more lateral exploration.
- **Acoustic/electronic coexistence:** balafon, sanza, marimba, harp, flutes and percussion retain recognizable identities alongside synthesizers. This is not simply acoustic playing processed into anonymous ambience.
- **A mobile jazz voice:** Bideau’s saxophone is described in the official notes as “half-narrator, half-explorer”—sometimes leading, sometimes disappearing into the rhythmic fabric.
- **Different kinds of trance:** the sources identify spiritual-jazz spaciousness in “A Waking Dream,” layered propulsion in “Anima,” flute/birdsong interlocking in “Drongo’s Flute,” and gnawa/Berlin School affinities in “Rain Dance.”
- **Documented influences versus critical comparisons:** Alice Coltrane, Pharoah Sanders and Sun Ra appear in the official notes. Harmonia, McCoy Tyner’s *Extensions* and Berlin kosmische are reviewer comparisons, not independently established statements of Bideau’s intent.

### The personnel network

Bideau composed, recorded and produced the album. Contributors include **Sarathy Korwar** on tabla/percussion, **Dudù Kouate** on percussion, flutes, ngoni, birdsongs and vocals, **Marysia Osu** on harp, **Daniel Brandt** on electronics for “Anima,” **Lluís Domènech Plana** on flutes/birdsongs, and **Mattias Mimoun** on piano/synths. Additional recordings: **Jan Brauer**; mixing: **Greg Freeman**; mastering: **Zino Mikorey**.

Those connections inform the shortlist—but musical fit, particularly your repeated “Drongo’s Flute” listening, determines the ranking.

## Ranked recommendations

### 1. Sarathy Korwar — *KALAK*
**2022 · The Leaf Label**  
**Entry track:** “Utopia Is A Colonial Project” — 4:17  
[Bandcamp: music and full credits](https://sarathykorwar.bandcamp.com/album/kalak) · [Qobuz](https://play.qobuz.com/album/thqbnctrz0bya) · [Label’s conceptual explanation](https://www.theleaflabel.com/sarathy-korwar-kalak/)

**Why it follows this album:** The strongest combined rhythmic and collaborator connection. Korwar’s circular notation system provided a framework for ensemble improvisation; **Photay** then arranged, produced and mixed the recordings into an electronic record. Percussion, Tamar Osborn’s saxophone/flute, synthesizers and communal vocals offer a particularly defensible continuation of “Drongo’s Flute,” “Rain Dance” and “Dawning Dusk.”

The connection is more specific than “both contain tabla”: *KALAK* treats **cyclicality itself as compositional architecture**, allowing ensemble interaction and electronic production to reinforce rather than replace one another.

**Interesting tension:** Its anti-colonial critique of utopia complicates the “unknown land” imagery surrounding Akusmi. That makes it a thoughtful companion rather than merely a sonic duplicate.

**Confidence:** Very high.  
**Caveat:** More explicitly political, with a stronger electronic-production identity.

### 2. Joshua Abrams & Natural Information Society — *Simultonality*
**2017 · Eremite / tak:til**  
**Entry track:** “Maroon Dune” — 9:07  
[Original European Bandcamp edition](https://simultonality.bandcamp.com/album/simultonality) · [Eremite Bandcamp](https://eremiterecords.bandcamp.com/album/simultonality) · [Detailed label notes](https://www.eremite.com/albums/mte-68)

**Why it follows this album:** Probably the best destination for the seed’s **patient but physically active repetition**. Guimbri, harmonium, electric autoharp, guitar and two drummers generate densely patterned motion without sacrificing a meditative centre.

The label provides unusually concrete rhythmic evidence: on **“Sideways Fall,” the drummers divide the “Vitamin C” beat into separate parts**, with one drummer in each stereo channel. That is a useful next step from Akusmi’s ensemble interlocking. Closing track “2128½” adds Ari Brown’s tenor saxophone and an explicitly documented Alice Coltrane connection.

**Why this particular NIS album:** Abrams calls it an album of “pure motion”; it is faster and more tightly structured than *Magnetoception*. That better matches the vitality of *Terra Incognita*.

**Confidence:** Very high.  
**Caveat:** Less synthesizer-oriented; single-take ensemble interaction replaces Bideau’s layered studio construction. The Eremite Bandcamp page displays a **2024 date**, but the original release is **2017**, verified by the earlier edition.

### 3. Francis Bebey — *Psychedelic Sanza 1982–1984*
**2014 compilation · recordings from 1982–1984 · Born Bad Records**  
**Entry track:** “Forest Nativity” — 4:23  
[Official Bandcamp](https://francisbebey.bandcamp.com/album/psychedelic-sanza-1982-1984) · [Qobuz catalog edition](https://play.qobuz.com/album/wx3oma0pozx53) · [Track-specific description and listing](https://bleep.com/release/54644-francis-bebey-psychedelic-sanza)

**Why it follows this album:** The most revealing unexpected pick. Bebey’s combination of **sanza, electronics, one-note flute and polyphony** offers a historical parallel to the small, bright instrumental voices and overlapping rhythms that make “Drongo’s Flute” distinctive.

Bebey’s son Patrick describes these recordings as his father’s private sonic “laboratory.” [In Sheep’s Clothing’s account](https://insheepsclothinghifi.com/album/francis-bebey-psychedelic-sanza-1982-1984/) specifically identifies electronic experimentation, sanza and flute/polyphonic traditions. This is a documented musical bridge—not a claim that Bideau directly borrowed from Bebey.

**Confidence:** High.  
**Caveat:** An archival compilation with vocals and older recording textures, not a contemporary jazz ensemble album. Qobuz misleadingly spells its title **“Psychedelic Zanzu 1982-1984”** and lists Ozileka; the verified tracklist matches the Born Bad compilation. Use Bandcamp for authoritative naming.

### 4. Daniel Brandt — *Channels*
**2018 · Erased Tapes**  
**Entry track:** “Daze” — 5:15  
[Bandcamp: music and extensive album notes](https://danielbrandt1.bandcamp.com/album/channels) · [Qobuz](https://play.qobuz.com/album/m74wneixrj56b)

**Why it follows this album:** This is a **two-way personnel connection**: Brandt supplies electronics on Akusmi’s “Anima,” while **Bideau co-wrote “Daze”** and played guitar/bass in Brandt’s touring ensemble.

The album notes explicitly identify Steve Reich’s *Six Pianos* and *Electric Counterpoint*, live-band development, orchestral detail and techno propulsion. “Daze” is the best entrance because its acoustic melody and percussion pulse expose the shared working method; follow it with “Flamingo” for the more club-facing side.

**Confidence:** High.  
**Caveat:** More techno-oriented and occasionally darker; fewer of the seed’s flute, harp and African instrumental colours.

### 5. Marysia Osu — *harp, beats & dreams*
**2024 · Brownswood Recordings**  
**Entry track:** “memento mori (feat. YUIS)” — 6:06  
[Bandcamp](https://marysiaosu.bandcamp.com/album/harp-beats-dreams) · [Qobuz](https://play.qobuz.com/album/qbz5z4q7d7kub) · [Brownswood’s detailed album account](https://brownswood.ochre.store/release/465636-marysia-osu-harp-beats-dreams)

**Why it follows this album:** Follow the actual harpist from “A Waking Dream,” “Pleine Lune” and “Drongo’s Flute” into her own production world. The chosen track combines **delay-treated harp with YUIS’s alto/concert flutes**, making the timbral connection unusually precise.

Osu composed, arranged and produced the homemade album herself. Its intimate acoustic details and electronic production provide a quieter answer to Bideau’s electroacoustic approach.

**Confidence:** High for timbre; medium-high overall.  
**Caveat:** A decompression route, not an equivalent dose of dense polyrhythmic propulsion.

### 6. Don Cherry — *Organic Music Society*
**1972 original · Caprice; linked digital reissue dated 2012**  
**Entry track:** “Terry’s Tune” — **track 12, the 5:10 version**  
[Qobuz](https://play.qobuz.com/album/ooocr5icpahpb) · [Caprice’s personnel, recording history and tracklist](https://old.capricemusic.se/capricerecords/artikel/organic-music-society/?lang=en)

**Why it follows this album:** A historical route into the seed’s **communal, border-crossing musical construction**. Cherry combines cornet, voice, harmonium and flute with musicians including Naná Vasconcelos, Okay Temiz and Christer Bothén. The repertoire directly joins **Terry Riley’s minimalism** with spiritual-jazz material, including “The Creator Has a Master Plan.”

The longer “Terry’s Tune” is a concise way into that intersection before undertaking the full album.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.  
**Caveat:** Loose live settings, chanting and heterogeneous recording circumstances make it less polished and less continuously groove-focused than Akusmi. This is a historical parallel, not a verified direct influence.

**For the closest next album:** start with ***KALAK***.

For a continuous exploratory sequence:

1. **KALAK** — keep the percussion/electronics dialogue moving.
2. **Simultonality** — hear interlocking rhythm sustained by a working ensemble.

4. **Channels** — turn toward the Bideau–Brandt minimalist production connection.
5. **harp, beats & dreams** — isolate harp, flute and intimate acoustic space.
6. **Organic Music Society** — finish with the older communal/minimalist intersection.
