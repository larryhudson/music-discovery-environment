# Saved albums — and why

Saved to Qobuz during our October 11, 2026 discovery session: 17 albums. These notes capture the recommendation rationale, not a rating or a claim that you will like each album. Qobuz is your listening shelf, not evidence of preference. Taste connections came primarily from repeated Last.fm listening. Recommendations were researched from credits, artist commentary and reviews, not firsthand audio auditioning.

## First round: new releases

### Jude Brothers — Dogwood Maiden Voyage
**Why saved:** Your Hana Stretton, Babehoven and Shannon Lay listening suggested intimate, handmade folk with room for strangeness. Harp, fiddle, acoustic guitar, wandering structures and environmental recordings make this the pastoral, acoustic branch—not a straightforward match to Pollett's electronic production.
**Try first:** Treat Me Like A Tree & Love Me For My Crookedness.
**Caveat:** Long and meandering; less suitable if you want concise songs or electric arrangements.

### Resavoir — Above the Clouds
**Why saved:** A bridge between your Akusmi, Jasmine Myra, Bonobo and Duffy/Uhlmann listening: improvised electronic jazz with dreamy textures and rhythmic movement. Gregory Uhlmann plays on it; Resavoir's Will Miller also contributes to Jeremiah Chiu's Edge Conditions, making this a collaborator-network discovery.
**Try first:** Stratosphere, then 4th Cloud.

### Margo Cilker — Little White Cross
**Why saved:** The grounded storytelling choice, prompted by your sustained Josaleigh Pollett listening plus Angela Autumn and Le Ren. Direct emotional writing, unhurried singing and pedal steel; originals and selected covers share a thread of grief and resilience.
**Try first:** Pauline for tenderness; State Line for movement.

### Strawberry Hands — A Jazz Blessing for the Animal
**Why saved:** An adventurous bridge from Hana Stretton, Lambchop and Asher White into bedroom-made psychedelic folk. Intimate songs become strange sound-worlds through layered voices, nylon-string guitar, pedal steel, strings and unstable production.
**Try first:** Dream of Water, then What Plant Jumps Over Me?
**Caveat:** A wildcard, not a safe genre match.

### BODEGA — All Inside Aquarium
**Why saved:** For the Sweeping Promises / Getdown Services / Westside Cowboy lane of your listening. A test of whether your taste for lively guitar bands extends to bigger, swaggering alternative rock.
**Try first:** Literary World, then Weather Me.
**Caveat:** This album moves away from the band's earlier clipped post-punk toward Jane's Addiction / Stone Roses territory.

### Holy Tongue — Basilikum
**Why saved:** A rhythm-first stretch from Akusmi and Flur toward dub, ritual percussion, vintage drum machines and spacious improvisation.
**Try first:** Coca; Orange 28 for a longer immersion.
**Caveat:** Darker and less immediately melodic than the taste anchors.

## After Akusmi — Terra Incognita

The seed signal was repeated listening to interlocking rhythms, flute/harp colour and gradual rhythmic emergence—especially Drongo's Flute and A Waking Dream—not simply an interest in saxophone jazz.

### Sarathy Korwar — KALAK
**Why saved:** The closest percussion/electronics continuation. Korwar plays on Terra Incognita; this record uses circular compositional structures and ensemble improvisation shaped by Photay's electronic production.
**Try first:** Utopia Is A Colonial Project.
**Caveat:** More explicitly political and electronically sculpted.

### Francis Bebey — Psychedelic Sanza 1982–1984
**Why saved:** A historical detour for the Drongo's Flute side of Akusmi: sanza, electronic experimentation, one-note flute and overlapping voices. A musical parallel, not a verified direct influence.
**Try first:** Forest Nativity.
**Catalog note:** Qobuz calls the matching release Psychedelic Zanzu 1982-1984.

### Daniel Brandt — Channels
**Why saved:** The minimalist-to-techno branch, with a concrete two-way connection: Brandt contributes electronics to Akusmi's Anima; Akusmi's Pascal Bideau co-wrote Daze.
**Try first:** Daze.
**Caveat:** More club-facing, with less flute/harp colour.

### Marysia Osu — harp, beats & dreams
**Why saved:** Follow Terra Incognita's actual harpist into her own intimate electronic production. Delay-treated harp and flute make the timbral connection particularly specific.
**Try first:** memento mori (feat. YUIS).
**Caveat:** A quieter decompression route, not the same dense rhythmic propulsion.

### Don Cherry — Organic Music Society
**Why saved:** The historical communal-trance route: voice, percussion, flute and harmonium, with an intersection between spiritual jazz and Terry Riley's minimalism. A historical parallel, not a verified Akusmi influence.
**Try first:** Terry's Tune, the 5:10 version.
**Caveat:** Looser and less polished than Akusmi.

## After Josaleigh Pollett — If I Let It Quiet

The seed signal was sustained attachment to the whole record. The target was emotionally direct songs whose electronic and environmental surroundings become tactile, unstable and meaningful—not generic folk similarity.

### Loma — Don't Shy Away
**Why saved:** The closest continuation of intimate singing, environmental sound and electronic movement. Jenn Wasner, named by Pollett as a reference point, contributes; Brian Eno developed Homing from emailed stems, offering a parallel to Pollett and Watko's long-distance collaboration.
**Try first:** Ocotillo, then Homing.
**Caveat:** More enigmatic and restrained lyrically.

### Beth Orton — Kidsticks
**Why saved:** A vulnerable, lived-in voice inside adventurous electronic rhythms, co-produced with Andrew Hung of Fuck Buttons. Treble's Pollett review explicitly names this album as a comparison.
**Try first:** Flesh and Blood.
**Caveat:** Brighter and more playful than Pollett.

### Sparklehorse — It's a Wonderful Life
**Why saved:** The older haunted-production branch: fragile singing, domestic unease, surreal imagery, humming keyboards, static and occasional abrasive guitar.
**Try first:** Apple Bed; Piano Fire for the louder side.
**Caveat:** More oblique and death-shadowed.

### phoneswithchords — piecemeal
**Why saved:** The direct collaborator and lyrical route: Pollett guests on put pressure. Slow songs about emotional defenses, old wounds, shame and unresolved forgiveness.
**Try first:** put pressure feat. Josaleigh Pollett.
**Caveat:** A closer match to the slow-song side than the seed's expansive electronic peaks.

### Low — Double Negative
**Why saved:** Follow Pollett's singing-through-interference and feedback into a record where distortion becomes compositional material. Pollett names Low as an influence, though not this specific album.
**Try first:** Always Trying to Work It Out.
**Caveat:** The most demanding, grimmest production-led recommendation here.

### Juana Molina — Un Día
**Why saved:** The loop-and-texture branch from Like a River, Repeating and the instrumental interludes: acoustic guitar and voice transformed into warm electronic patterns and polyrhythms.
**Try first:** Los Hongos de Marosa.
**Caveat:** Stronger production affinity than lyrical continuity; Spanish and nonverbal vocals.
