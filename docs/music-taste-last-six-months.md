# Music taste profile: last six months

Prepared: 11 October 2026. Compare with [the overall profile](music-taste-overall.md).

## Summary

**Close-voiced songwriting and enveloping arrangements, with renewed live-band energy.** Compared with the all-time profile, the current center of gravity has moved away from jagged guitar catharsis toward intimate songs, detailed electroacoustic production, homespun ensemble chemistry, and some patient instrumental exploration.

But these six months contain two different phases:

1. **April to early July:** an unusually strong immersion in Gia Margaret's catalog, including both vocal songs and instrumental records.
2. **Mid-July to October:** Josaleigh Pollett, Babehoven, and Westside Cowboy lead, with Johanna Samuels continuing across both phases. The emphasis is not simply “ambient”: it includes electronically embellished folk songs and energetic, unforced band performances.

The durable thread is still character, emotional specificity, and interesting arrangements. The preferred delivery has changed much more than the underlying values.

## Scope and method

Exact window: **11 April 2026 at 00:00:00 UTC through 11 October 2026 at 18:38:41 UTC**, inclusive start and exclusive end. This is six calendar months plus the current day's elapsed hours, not Last.fm's approximate `6month` chart preset. Completed scrobbles only; now-playing was excluded.

Fetched every page of that window and aggregated its contents:

- **6,267 plays** on **155 distinct UTC dates**.
- **858 source artist names**.
- **1,246 named artist/album pairs**; 10 plays have no album tag.
- **3,323 distinct source artist/track pairs**.
- Top 20 source artist names account for approximately **35.1%** of plays.

The descriptive charts below retain source spellings. Selected artist comparisons use case-insensitive matching, but no comprehensive artist-alias reconciliation was attempted. Distinct dates establish repeated recorded listening, not the context or level of attention involved.

Descriptions of the music are grounded in the reviews linked below. The taste conclusions are analytical inferences, not claims of personal audition. Scrobbles are not ratings: a high count establishes repeat listening, not an explicit verdict on every song. Nothing here diagnoses the listener's emotional state or assumes why the music was played.

Raw listening history and research snapshots remain outside the repository in `/persistent/music-research-2026-10-11/`. This document contains only summary evidence and interpretation.

## Principal taste dimensions

### 1. Intimate voice and atmosphere now occupy the center

Gia Margaret leads at 445 plays, spread across four major album tags:

- *Singing*: 204.
- *Romantic Piano*: 94.
- *There's Always Glimmer*: 74.
- *Mia Gargaret*: 65.

*Singing* appears on 29 separate UTC dates and all 12 of its track names occur in the window. “E-Motion” is the most-played track overall, with 32 plays; “Alive Inside” has 20 and “Moon Not Mine” 19.

[Linnie Greene's Pitchfork review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/gia-margaret-singing/) describes close, warm vocals, acoustic guitar, modular synthesizers, analog drum machines, and textured arrangements. [Will Yarbrough in The Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/albums/gia-margaret-singing-tests-her-range) emphasizes the relationship between Margaret's singing and the production language developed during her instrumental work. Both reviews are mixed rather than uniformly enthusiastic; their descriptions are useful without treating their verdicts as yours.

**Interpretation:** the leading recent signal is not “no vocals” or “background music.” It is the meeting of a specific voice with an enveloping production world. The catalog breadth makes a purely ambient reading inadequate, while the timing below prevents treating Gia as the latest dominant fixation.

### 2. Folk songs with electronic detail are a particularly strong current bridge

Josaleigh Pollett has 203 plays, 188 of them tagged to *If I Let It Quiet*. That album was played on 17 distinct dates, with all 12 track names represented. “The Witness” has 20 plays, “Bed Of Quiet” 19, and “Like A River” 18.

[Dom Lepore's Treble review](https://www.treblezine.com/josaleigh-pollett-if-i-let-it-quiet-review/) describes a synthesis of acoustic folk and indietronica: digitally processed grooves, samples, reversed piano, cello textures, breakbeats, synth arpeggios, and occasional roaring feedback, held together by a gentle voice and underlying folk songs.

**Interpretation:** your recent taste is open to significant production intervention when the song remains emotionally legible. “Quiet” should not be confused with bare acoustic arrangements, and “electronic” need not mean a separate dance-music category. This is one of the clearest routes for future recommendations.

### 3. Human ensemble chemistry remains important, with more rootsy and live-band expressions

Babehoven has 155 plays, including 121 for *I See Them, I See Me*. Its 14 track names occur across eight listening dates. Westside Cowboy has 113 plays, 112 of them for *It Goes On*, across 12 dates.

[Grant Sharples in Paste](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/babehoven/babehoven-i-see-them-i-see-me-review) describes Babehoven's fuller live-band feel, prominent fiddles, homespun alt-country colors, and thoughtful observational songwriting. He also questions whether the strongest country signifiers always help; this is evidence of a particular arrangement palette, not a reason to relabel your entire taste as country.

[Victoria Borlando's Pitchfork review of Westside Cowboy](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/westside-cowboy-it-goes-on/) stresses pummeling drums, amped jangle, four-part harmonies, raw band chemistry, and changes in texture, volume, and tempo. The review explicitly cautions against reducing the group to its early country branding.

**Interpretation:** you are not only moving toward softer music. Energy now often arrives through musicians playing together, vocal interaction, and unforced dynamics rather than the more severe abrasion of the all-time noise/post-punk anchors. This connects to the longstanding Ocean Party preference rather than replacing it.

### 4. Warm, harmonically interesting songwriting has moved forward

Johanna Samuels has 137 plays. *Sorry, Kid* has 81, across 12 listening dates. “White Limousine” has 20 plays, “Two People, the Moon” 18, “Blue vs. Pinkerton” 17, and “Circles” 16. Some repeated tracks can carry different release tags; these track counts should not be equated directly with plays tagged to *Sorry, Kid*.

[Rachel Alm's Spectrum Culture review](https://spectrumculture.com/2026/09/20/johanna-samuels-sorry-kid-review/) describes concise, harmonically interesting songs, warm analog textures, Wurlitzer/Mellotron, close harmonies, and collaborative contributions that do not overpower the songs.

**Interpretation:** the appeal of understated observation survives, but the current examples need not have the sparseness or miniature scale of Frankie Cosmos. Richer harmony and studio detail are promising if they support, rather than obscure, the individual voice.

### 5. A secondary exploratory lane values immersion and gradual unfolding

Akusmi has 55 plays, including 53 for *Terra Incognita*, across 12 artist-listening dates. Jasmine Myra has 44 plays, all tagged to *Where Light Settles*, across seven dates. Hana Stretton has 74 plays, including 50 for *tiarn*, across six artist-listening dates.

[Dash Lewis's Akusmi review](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/akusmi-terra-incognita/) describes airy textures, layered ensemble sound, slowly coalescing rhythms, and hypnotic grooves. [Everything Is Noise on Jasmine Myra](https://everythingisnoise.net/reviews/jasmine-myra-where-light-settles/) emphasizes patient, textured jazz arrangements and restrained playing. [Sam Goldner on Hana Stretton](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/hana-stretton-tiarn/) describes impressionistic folk, environmental sound, subtle instrumental details, and songs gradually coming into focus.

**Interpretation:** repetition, space, and the way a piece develops can substitute for sharp structural conflict. This is a real secondary lane, not the majority of listening, and not necessarily passive listening.

An important correction to the earlier 12-month-based impressions: **Ann Annie has only four plays and Nala Sinephro five in this exact six-month window**. They should not be used as leading evidence for this profile merely because they ranked strongly across the previous year.

**444 of Gia Margaret's 445 plays occur in the first segment.** In contrast, all 203 Josaleigh Pollett plays, 123 of 155 Babehoven plays, and 112 of 113 Westside Cowboy plays occur in the second.

For recommendations right now, weight **Josaleigh Pollett, Babehoven, Westside Cowboy, Johanna Samuels, and the later-period exploratory records** more heavily than the six-month Gia total alone. The earlier Gia immersion remains valuable context for compatible production and intimacy, not a guarantee of the latest priority.

Month totals range from 472 plays in July to 1,499 in August. April and October are partial months. Unequal listening volumes and differing release availability mean neither monthly counts nor rankings should be treated as controlled tests of preference.

## Guidance for future recommendations

### Weight more heavily now

- Close, identifiable voices with emotionally specific songs.
- Folk songwriting strengthened by electronic textures, sampling, or subtle rhythm programming.
- Warm harmonic detail and collaborative vocal arrangements.
- Raw live-band chemistry, jangle, dynamic changes, and grounded observational writing.
- Patient, immersive pieces whose small details develop over time.
- New artist voices rather than only adjacent releases by historical favorites.

### Retain as secondary routes

- Noisy guitar music with emotional range or unusual instrumental interplay.
- Rhythmic agitation, wit, and energetic band performances.
- The Australian guitar-pop and understated ensemble preferences of the overall profile.

### Avoid overinterpreting

- Do not use Pile's all-time dominance as the main current recommendation filter.
- Do not call the entire period ambient, acoustic-only, or country.
- Do not assume low recent counts mean former favorites are now disliked.
- Do not let Gia's early-period concentration obscure the latest three months.
- Do not infer life events, mental health, or listening purpose from the music's emotional tone.

**One-line current brief:** intimate songs with a distinctive voice, enveloping but purposeful arrangements, and human ensemble warmth—open to electronics, rootsy detail, and renewed guitar energy, with less emphasis on relentless abrasion.
