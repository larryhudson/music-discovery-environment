# Overall music taste profile

Updated: 11 October 2026. Based on the all-time Last.fm charts reviewed on that date.

## Summary

**Intimacy with friction:** emotionally specific, idiosyncratic songwriting; guitars with tension and character; arrangements that reward repeated listening; melancholy without inflated presentation; both angular propulsion and patiently suspended space.

The most useful recommendation target is not simply “indie rock” or “noise rock.” It is music that combines an identifiable human voice with interesting musical relationships—between instruments, between quiet and loud passages, or between modest presentation and substantial emotional weight.

This is an analytical profile, not a claim that every favorite fits one aesthetic. The top 20 artists account for approximately 30.4% of all recorded plays; there is considerable exploration beyond this center.

## Evidence and scope

The research used:

- All-time top artists: first 150 entries.
- Complete all-time album chart: 12,317 entries.
- Complete all-time track chart: approximately 29,770 entries.
- Approximately 119,764 total scrobbles and 6,747 distinct artists reported by the account.
- Online reviews of representative favorites to ground descriptions of their music.

“All-time” means Last.fm's overall chart, not a verified five-year date window. The listener describes the meaningful listening period as approximately five years; the account itself was registered in August 2015. Registration does not establish when active or consistent scrobbling began. No complete lifetime history was downloaded to resolve that distinction.

Counts are a snapshot, not an atomic transaction, and can move during research. Scrobbles measure recorded plays, not ratings, attentive listening, purchases, or all listening outside Last.fm. Artist and release aliases can split counts; chart spellings are generally retained below.

## Principal taste dimensions

### 1. Tenderness inside structurally restless music

**Evidence:** Pile is the leading artist with 6,990 plays, versus 2,657 for second-place Frankie Cosmos. *Green and Gray* is the top album at 1,837 plays. Other heavily played Pile albums include *You're Better Than This* (950), *A Hairshirt of Purpose* (902), *Dripping* (837), and *Magic Isn't Real* (782). “Firewood” is the leading track with 179 plays.

Zach Schonfeld's [Pitchfork review of *Green and Gray*](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/pile-green-and-gray/) describes odd meters, pummeling drums, serrated riffs, keyboard/orchestral depth, and tenderness preceding crashing climaxes.

**Interpretation:** intensity matters, but it is not reducible to loudness. The emotional and atmospheric sides of this music are integral to the preference. A recommendation that offers abrasion without expressive range is a weaker match than one that makes tension meaningful.

### 2. Small songs with substantial emotional lives

**Evidence:** Frankie Cosmos (2,657), Lomelda (1,944), Florist (1,517), and Free Cake for Every Creature (1,141) are major repeat listens. Lomelda's *Hannah* is the second-place album (1,248); *Talking Quietly of Anything With You* has 931 plays; *Next Thing* has 840.

[Will Gottsegen's review of *Hannah*](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/lomelda-hannah/) emphasizes connection, communication, perseverance, and sadness that retains a glow. [Kevin Lozano's review of *Next Thing*](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/21765-next-thing/) describes understated guitars, matter-of-fact, off-center delivery, and short songs with the density of intimate conversation.

**Interpretation:** vocal character, modest scale, and precise observation appear more useful recommendation criteria than vocal grandeur or conspicuous polish. Brief, informal-seeming songs can support deep attachment if their details remain compelling.

### 3. Unforced melancholy and ensemble chemistry

**Evidence:** The Ocean Party (2,364), Snowy Band (1,351), Ciggie Witch (1,173), The Finks (1,008), and Pop Filter (867) form a substantial Australian guitar-pop cluster. *The Oddfellows' Hall* has 677 plays, *Audio Commentary* 735, and *Classic Connection* 778.

[Beat's review of *The Oddfellows' Hall*](https://beat.com.au/the-ocean-partys-the-oddfellows-hall-embodies-the-genius-and-dynamism-of-zac-denton/) describes emotional depth without overwrought presentation, shared songwriting, everyday anxieties, and an enduring earnestness.

**Interpretation:** unforced ensemble music, everyday melancholy, and the feeling of musicians making space for one another are particularly promising. This cluster is too prominent to treat as incidental geography or a single-band preference.

### 4. Nervous rhythmic intelligence

**Evidence:** Pardoner (2,041), Truth Club (1,723), Uranium Club (1,519), Stuck (1,497), and Landowner (967) form another strong cluster. *Came Down Different* has 1,135 plays; *Change Is Bad* has 1,060; Truth Club's *Running From the Chase* and *Not An Exit* have 794 and 778 respectively.

Reviews of Stuck's *Optimizer* in [Treble](https://www.treblezine.com/stuck-optimizer-review/) and Landowner's *Assumption* in [Pitchfork](https://pitchfork.com/reviews/albums/landowner-assumption/) emphasize knotty but melodic arrangements and minimalist rhythmic precision respectively. These newer reviews provide context for the artists, not proof that every older favorite has exactly the same sound.

**Interpretation:** agitation works especially well when it has shape, propulsion, restraint, or wit. Simply matching an “angular post-punk” tag is less useful than checking how the rhythm section, melodies, and vocal perspective interact.

### 5. Restraint and patient instrumental relationships

**Evidence:** The New Year (1,419) and Bedhead (1,119) are strong all-time anchors. *Newness Ends* has 908 plays and *Beheaded* 500. Hovvdy is third overall with 2,405 plays, and Charlie Martin has 984.

[Exclaim's *Newness Ends* review](https://exclaim.ca/music/article/new_year-newness_ends) contrasts the Kadane brothers' slow, melancholy melodic strengths with the new band's faster passages. The critic is skeptical of the quicker material; that verdict does not override the listener's strong attachment to the album.

**Interpretation:** quiet is not merely a break between heavier favorites. Space, subdued delivery, and patient instrumental relationships are substantial preferences in their own right. A climactic payoff need not be mandatory.

## Recommendation guidance

### Strong positive signals

- An identifiable, idiosyncratic voice rather than interchangeable genre styling.
- Specific observations, conversational lyrics, or emotionally meaningful fragments.
- Melodies with personality, including those embedded in unusual structures.
- Guitar/bass/drum interplay, dynamic tension, or carefully deployed space.
- Modest presentation that reveals more with repetition.
- Emotional weight without compulsory theatricality.
- Abrasiveness paired with rhythmic purpose, tenderness, humor, or atmosphere.

### Useful bridges

- Intimate songwriting inside noisy or structurally restless arrangements.
- Informal guitar pop with thoughtful ensemble interplay.
- Restrained songs with subtle rhythmic or harmonic complexity.
- Angular music whose social observation is sharpened by wit or character.

### Weaker bets—not established dislikes

- Generic abrasion without melodic, emotional, or rhythmic distinction.
- Genre resemblance based only on an artist-name comparison.
- Grand presentation that obscures specific human detail.
- A polished sound offered as its own justification.

The charts do not establish that these qualities are disliked. They are lower-confidence recommendation routes given the strongest repeat-listening evidence.
